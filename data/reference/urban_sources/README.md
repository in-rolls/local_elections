# Urban election source coverage

This inventory distinguishes inspected data from sources still to be acquired. It was compiled November 8–9, 2026, to identify urban election histories beyond TCPD/LokDhaba. [coverage.csv](coverage.csv) records sources and design constraints. Years in `cycles_to_investigate` are search targets, not a claim of complete data. [inspected_inputs.csv](inspected_inputs.csv) pins the initial inputs; [acquisition_log.json](acquisition_log.json) records the initial source search. Each subsequent handoff has its own acquisition receipts and checksums. Original data remain in their upstream repositories; this inventory does not copy them.

## Completed source passes

- **Chandigarh:** [The official-report handoff](../../chandigarh/source_review.md) contains 26 winners each in 2006 and 2011 and 35 in 2021. The October 7, 2011 administration letter explicitly retains the 2006 ward boundaries. Candidate honorifics provide separately recorded sex for every 2011 winner. Among 17 later nonquota wards, nine were previously reserved for women and eight were not. The parser separates a 2009 by-election replacement from the 2006 general-election winner. The typed 26-ward panel supports a conditional persistence comparison; the source report documents the lotteries, rotation exclusions, caste-population rankings, and complete candidate-honorific outcomes. The 2021 gazette lacks winner sex and has no validated historical geographic link.
- **Kerala:** [The corporation source review](../../../../local_elections_kerala/data/search_log/sec/urban_review/README.md) covers 1,608 ward-years through 2025. It retains 414 provisional winners in 2015, 411 in 2020, and 420 in 2025. Recorded profile sex links to 197 and 161 provisional winners in 2015 and 2020. Later member profiles demonstrably include by-election replacements. The 2020 candidate PDF's gender field also conflicts with independently held profiles. Boundary sources support 718 candidate links, but remaining ward-name differences, profile timing, and incomplete outcome linkage prevent treating these as a validated panel.
- **UP corporations:** [The source supplement](../../../../local_elections_up/data/reference/urban_council_sources/README.md) adds 210 official 2023 winner records: all 110 Lucknow wards, all 60 Ayodhya wards, and 40 of 90 Aligarh wards. Sex is printed for the 100 Ayodhya/Aligarh records; Lucknow's PDF supplies names and parties only. Nine archived captures repeat the same first page of the SEC table, rather than extending its coverage. The existing 2,280 winners from 2012 and 2017 reproduce their retained HTML. Three reservation-source conflicts and source municipality-label errors are documented separately. A 2017 Lucknow delimitation notification rules out assuming geographic continuity from equal ward counts.
- **Kolkata:** [The 2015 handoff](../../west_bengal/release/kolkata/2015/README.md) supplies all 144 official winner records from eight party lists. Names agree with TCPD after whitespace normalization, though these sources can share a common origin. The original winner gazette and KMC oath list are retained as supporting sources; full gazette transcription is not claimed. Neither the primary winner pages nor TCPD supplies recorded winner sex. TCPD reservation labels remain separate pending primary verification. No historical geographic transition has been validated.

- **Patna:** [The two-wave handoff](../../../../local_elections_bihar/data/reference/urban/patna/README.md) contains 75 official winners in each of 2017 and 2022, plus 1,008 TCPD candidates for 2017. A signed reservation notification resolves two discrepancies in the 2017 winner grid. All 75 reservation categories persist into 2022, so this pair supplies no quota exits and no post-quota persistence comparison. Both sources record sex, but one 2017 reserved-ward winner has a conflicting male label that remains flagged. The 2022 API age is dynamic, not election age; education is unavailable. Geographic equivalence remains unverified.

- **Hyderabad:** [The GHMC handoff](../../../../local_elections_telangana/data/release/urban/ghmc/README.md) contains 300 winners across 2016 and 2020, 1,333 candidates in 2016, and 1,122 official candidate profiles in 2020 with sex, age and education. A separate 2020 return covers 1,117 candidates in 149 wards; ward 136 is absent from that PDF but present in the complete profiles and winner gazette. Official orders retain both boundaries and reservations between 2016 and 2020, so the pair supplies no quota exits.
- **Chennai and Coimbatore:** [The Tamil Nadu handoff](../../../../local_elections_tamil_nadu/data/release/urban/README.md) contains 5,920 candidates and 500 winners: Chennai 2011 and 2022, and Coimbatore 2022. Explicit sex is available in the secondary 2022 extracts. Primary Chennai gazettes correct two reservation labels and document redelimitation; historical ward continuity is not assumed.
- **Bhubaneswar, Cuttack and Berhampur:** [The Odisha handoff](../../../../local_elections_odisha/data/release/urban/README.md) contains 294 winners across 2014 and 2022. Official 2022 reservation is available, but winner sex is not yet measured. A complete Bhubaneswar affidavit-link frame and 55 downloaded documents (54 unique files) for 42 winners are retained; those PDFs are not yet extracted attributes.
- **Bhopal and Indore:** [The MP handoff](../../madhya_pradesh/release/urban/README.md) contains 170 provisional 2022 winners from contemporaneous news lists, plus a separate official current Indore council roster and a dated municipal disclosure. The news lists lack sex, quota and votes. Current council membership is not substituted for election-time winners.

The Gujarat, other Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Karnataka, and Howrah entries remain source leads unless the CSV explicitly records an inspected source. A located URL is not a recovered dataset.

## Use in analysis

For post-quota persistence, election outcomes must be joined to dated reservation rosters and comparable electoral geography, with winner sex measured separately from reservation. A ward number or name match is not proof of unchanged boundaries. Among later nonquota wards, the comparison is prior quota assignment versus prior nonquota assignment; women who won nonquota seats remain in the comparison group. Multi-member systems, such as Surat's two women-reserved seats in every four-seat ward, require a separately defined exposure. Current-quota comparisons of education or other characteristics are separate analyses. No new effects or pooled estimates are produced by this inventory.

## Storage and schema

The Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Odisha and MP handoffs follow the shared [column vocabulary](../../../docs/naming.md) and [stage layout](../../../docs/layout.md). Original documents stay upstream in `data/raw/`; hand-entered readings and correction inputs belong in `data/manual/`; acquisition attempts and search notes belong in `data/search_log/`. Rebuildable tables and their contracts, dictionaries and checksums live in `data/release/urban/` (under the state folder for MP). Acquisition code and parsing code remain distinct in the state repositories.

Derived data tables are Parquet. Identifiers such as `ward_no` and `candidate_no` are strings; `reserved_woman`, `candidate_woman` and `winner_woman` are nullable Int8 indicators. Printed labels, unreported values and conflicting sources remain distinguishable. Candidate tables, election winners, current council rosters and affidavit documents each retain their own row unit and key. Generated dictionaries and schemas describe the same contract, and release verification checks the new files. These supplements are not yet pooled analysis inputs.

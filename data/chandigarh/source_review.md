# Chandigarh municipal election records

The release contains 87 ward winners: 26 for the 2006 council cohort, 26 for 2011, and 35 for 2021. It also contains all 152 candidates in the official 2011 candidate list, the separately printed ward-level gender counts, and a 26-ward panel linking 2006 to 2011. Original reports remain under `raw/`; every selected row has its PDF page and source path. [The release manifest](release/manifest.json) pins input and output hashes and schemas. [The dictionary](release/dictionary.csv) describes every column and its missingness.

## Sources and dates

The 2006 and 2011 official election reports were already held in this repository. Their original acquisition dates were not recorded and are not reconstructed. The current official [SEC catalog](https://chandigarh.gov.in/state-election-commission) lists historical reports. The December 28, 2021 [official gazette](https://egazette.chd.gov.in/OPENFILE.aspx?ID=1060+&etype=Notice) was acquired directly from the government gazette service; its [receipt](reference/winner_gazette_2021_receipt.json) records the URL, retrieval time, byte count and SHA-256. Only its first four pages concern these election results.

The 2006 report covers both the general election and subsequent by-elections. Its final winner table incorporates the 2009 replacement in ward 10. The release selects the contemporaneous 2006 winner, Gurnam Singh. Ward 7's election was deferred after a candidate's death; its winner belongs to the 2006 council cohort but actually polled on January 14, 2007. The report's deferred-result notification heading incorrectly predates that poll. The 2011 winner table also incorrectly labels its heading 2006; the 2011 result summary and dated notification establish the correct event. These discrepancies and the two reviewed spelling aliases are recorded in [the source-review ledger](reference/source_review.csv).

## Sex and reservation are separate measurements

The 2011 candidate list prints sex-specific honorifics and separate male/female totals by ward. `Sh.` is classified as male; `Smt.` and `Ms.` as female. Those classifications agree with the printed totals wherever all titles are sex-specific. One candidate uses `Dr.` alone, so her individual classification remains missing; the source's separate aggregate count is retained. No candidate or winner is classified from a personal name or a seat's reservation.

Every 2011 winner links uniquely to a candidate within the same ward, after normalizing capitalization and punctuation and applying the explicitly reviewed Modgil/Moudgil spelling alias. All 26 winners thus have an honorific-based classification measured separately from reservation. These records are different tables in the same official report, not independent publishers. The 2006 and 2021 selected winner tables do not provide equivalent information: sex remains missing for 25 of the 26 earlier winners and all 35 winners in 2021. The deferred ward-7 notification prints a sex-specific honorific. A missing sex is not a male observation.

## Geographic linkage and the research comparison

The Chandigarh Administration's October 7, 2011 letter, reproduced on PDF page 15 (printed page 13) of the 2011 report, explicitly retains the 26-ward profile established by the October 25, 2006 notification. This is the basis for linking 2006 to 2011 by ward number. The join uses the 26 outcome-election wards as its left universe, requires a unique prior record for each, and preserves all 26. There are no unmatched wards in either direction. There are 17 later non-quota wards: nine were reserved for women in the prior cohort and eight were not. The comparison retains women who win non-quota seats.

The panel records the quota assignment, not exposure to a female winner: women winning non-quota wards remain in the comparison group. Both caste reservation and women's reservation are retained. The 2016 extension below retains these same 26 wards; no link to the 2021 geography is asserted. The 2021 council has 35 wards, and its winner gazette does not resolve continuity with the earlier 26-ward geography.

## Reproduction and checks

From the repository root, run `python -m local_elections.states.chandigarh.parse` with `src` on the Python path, then `pytest tests/test_chandigarh.py -q`. Parsing is offline. Checks cover unique ward and candidate coverage; all printed male/female totals; independent reservation lists; winner/candidate name joins; vote margins; the deferred poll; the mixed-vintage table; the 2021 signature overlay; Parquet types, nulls and round trips; and source/output hashes. The 2021 file has no votes, and the releases contain no education or age measurements.


## Assignment rules and the eligible comparison

The signed October 14, 2011 letter No. 6/1/45/FII(8)-2011/8770 reproduces the methodology prescribed on October 27, 2006. It is scanned on **PDF pages 19–20 (printed pages 17–18)** of the [official 2011 election report](https://chandigarh.gov.in/files/documents/election-report-2011.pdf); its content was checked visually because PDF text extraction misses it. The currently served PDF matches the held original byte for byte; the [verification receipt](raw/election_report_2011_verification.json) records that check without inventing an earlier acquisition date. The [manual source reading](manual/assignment_rules.json) identifies the document, pages, source hash and substantive rules.

The letter specifies nine women-reserved wards chosen by lottery. Previously women-reserved wards are excluded from subsequent draws until all wards have been covered. SC reservation follows a different rule: the five wards with the highest 2001 Census SC population percentages are selected first, then the next five at the following election. The SC-women seats are selected by a separate public lottery within the five SC wards. The October 21, 2011 SEC order, PDF page 26 (printed page 24), confirms the public draw and records its results. The 2006 pre-election notification is on PDF page 77 of that year's report.

The panel parses both pre-election reservation notifications and checks them against the result tables. The census table on PDF page 25 of the 2011 report reproduces ward totals and SC populations supplied by the Deputy Commissioner. Its rankings exactly reproduce the five SC wards in 2006 and the next five in 2011. Wards 4 and 18 tie at rank 15; they remain tied, and the tie does not affect either election's SC assignments. Population and SC share are retained as pre-treatment context, with shares expressed as proportions.

The 17 wards open to both sexes in 2011 include all nine wards reserved for women in 2006 and eight other wards. All 17 have a winner honorific measured independently of the reservation label; there are no missing outcomes or unmatched wards. The caste-transition groups have treatment overlap:

| Caste reservation, 2006 → 2011 | Prior women-reserved | Prior non-quota |
|---|---:|---:|
| SC → NONE | 2 | 1 |
| NONE → SC | 2 | 1 |
| NONE → NONE | 5 | 6 |

The primary comparison is the equal-ward difference in the 2011 woman-winner rate between prior quota and prior non-quota wards, restricted to 2011 non-quota wards. Because current-open eligibility depends on earlier reservation and the interacting caste-specific lotteries, this is a conditional contrast. The lottery evidence is stronger than an assumption based on observed balance, but it does not justify treating the nine prior-quota assignments among these 17 wards as an unrestricted randomized assignment. Detailed ballot-level records and the ordering or rejection protocol for interacting draws are not supplied. An analysis should report this conditional contrast and can adjust for the three predetermined caste-transition strata, all of which contain both arms. Their small sizes limit precision. An unrestricted permutation test is not supported by the documented mechanism. Outcomes had already been inspected when this panel specification was developed.

## Panel contract and reproducibility

Use `release/ward_panel_2006_2011.parquet`, filtering `is_eligible`. The treatment is `reserved_woman_2006`; the outcome is `winner_woman_from_honorific_2011`. The latter is explicitly a reading of the separately printed candidate honorific, linked uniquely within ward; it is not a recorded sex cell or a classification from personal name. `Sh.` maps to 0 and `Smt.`/`Ms.` to 1. The one candidate with `Dr.` alone remains unclassified in the candidate table and is not a winner. No unknown value is recoded as zero.

The panel keeps all 26 outcome wards as its left universe and checks both wave keys before joining 1:1. This fixes an earlier implementation that constructed the prior-wave dictionary before checking duplicates and could silently overwrite a repeated prior ward. The revised check rejects duplicates before any dictionary is built. Each row retains the original report paths and hashes, reservation-notification pages, winner and honorific pages, the unchanged-boundary letter page and allocation-rule page. `source_path_2011` supplies the report for the census, boundary and allocation-rule page fields.

The [declared table schema](release/ward_panel_2006_2011.schema.json), [dictionary](release/dictionary.csv), [join and coverage audit](release/panel_audit.json), and [recode ledger](manual/panel_recodes.csv) travel with the data. Ward identifiers are strings; treatment and measured outcomes are nullable Int8 0/1/null, with this complete panel checked to contain no missing treatment or outcome. `NONE` denotes no caste reservation. Every previous panel value was compared through the recode map: all 26 rows retain their original assignments and outcomes. The existing source winner and candidate exports retain their earlier schemas in this focused panel revision. The release manifest pins the panel and generated metadata; parser startup verifies held source hashes before rebuilding.

## Extension through 2016

The [2011–2016 panel](release/ward_panel_2011_2016.parquet) adds all 26 wards on the same geography. The September 19, 2016 administration letter No. 6/1/45/FII(8)-2016/8768, reproduced on PDF pages 9–10 of the [official 2016 report](https://chandigarh.gov.in/files/GEMCC-2021/election-report-2016.pdf), explicitly retains the October 25, 2006 boundaries used in both earlier elections. Those signed pages were read visually; the narrative on PDF page 6 independently states the same continuity. Ward 7 had a repoll at polling station 14 on December 19, 2016, after the December 18 general poll. The panel retains both dates.

The 2016 order starts reservation afresh using Census 2011 SC shares. The five highest-share wards receive SC reservation; women’s seats are allocated by public draw, including a separate draw of two within those five SC wards. The signed letter describes exclusions for subsequent elections; the report’s page 6 states that 2016 starts afresh, and the October 14 allocation on PDF page 20 includes three wards also reserved for women in 2011. The 2016 SC wards are 5, 6, 7, 11 and 23. This is a restart of the earlier rotation, not a continuation to the third set of SC wards ranked on Census 2001. The [manual legal-source record](manual/assignment_rules_2016.json) pins the pages, dates, and distinctions.

Every elected person in detailed returns on PDF pages 161–168 matches the December 20 winner notification on pages 177–180 and an accepted nomination with an explicit candidate honorific on pages 96–123. `winner_woman_from_honorific_2016` records that title; `winner_woman_2016` stays null because no individual sex cell was printed. In ward 1, Mahesh Inder Singh in the result/notification corresponds to Maheshinder Singh Sidhu in the nomination list, corroborated by ward, party, and the same house address. The candidate title is read from the candidate column, not the adjacent father/husband column. Wrapped names are checked. The original nomination heading mistakenly prints the 2011 election date; all 26 names correspond to the separately dated 2016 returns. The parser also preserves the discrepancy between a summary’s 186 “contesting” candidates and the detailed returns’ 122 candidates plus 26 NOTA rows. It reconciles the latter’s votes to every ward total.

There are 17 later nonquota wards: six previously reserved for women and 11 previously nonreserved. **No woman won any of those 17 wards**, based on separately printed honorifics in every case. This is an observed zero in both groups, not complete information about the underlying probabilities. An OLS variance estimate collapses to zero here and must not give this comparison infinite precision in a synthesis. Interval estimation must accommodate zero events in both groups.

The 2011 assignment excluded all wards reserved for women in 2006. Four of the 11 later-open controls therefore had an earlier reserved term in 2006; the other seven were nonquota in both 2006 and 2011. All six treated wards had been nonquota in 2006. The panel retains that full history and provides `is_eligible_without_2006_quota` for the 13 later-open wards in the 2011 lottery risk pool. That restriction leaves six treated and seven controls, again with no woman winners. It changes the treatment-history comparison and should be labeled separately.

| Caste reservation, 2011 → 2016 | Prior women-reserved | Prior nonquota | Nonquota controls also nonquota in 2006 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| NONE → NONE | 3 | 8 | 6 |
| NONE → SC | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| SC → NONE | 1 | 2 | 0 |

All three caste-transition strata have both arms in the full 17-ward comparison. After the 2006-history restriction, SC → NONE has no controls; an adjusted model must not silently extrapolate a within-stratum comparison there. The ordinary full-sample contrast is conditional on current nonquota status and mixes earlier histories. The statutory lottery evidence is relevant, but the held sources do not supply ballot-level records or a complete ordering protocol for the interacting caste-specific draws. Neither comparison supports an unrestricted permutation test. Repeated observations of the same wards and common city assignment rules also preclude treating the two Chandigarh cycles as independent cities.

The extension uses the earlier 26-ward panel as its left-key reference, rejects duplicated keys before constructing a lookup, and preserves all 26 wards in a 1:1 join. Prior fields, report paths and hashes are carried forward unchanged. Its [schema](release/ward_panel_2011_2016.schema.json), [dictionary](release/ward_panel_2011_2016_dictionary.csv), [join audit](release/ward_panel_2011_2016_audit.json), and [manifest](release/ward_panel_2011_2016_manifest.json) document the extension separately from the original release tables. The ordinary `python -m local_elections.states.chandigarh.parse` command now builds both panels; the extension alone can be rebuilt with `python -m local_elections.states.chandigarh.extension`. Run both `tests/test_chandigarh.py` and `tests/test_chandigarh_extension.py` for affected checks.
